Magpie

A journal that can prove itself.

For the disputes life actually throws at people: the landlord who never fixed it, the messages that keep coming, the package that arrived broken. Magpie keeps photos, notes, and files encrypted on your device, and chains every entry to the last so the record cannot be quietly rewritten later. By anyone. You included, and that is the point.

Create your journal

There is no reset and no recovery. If this passphrase is lost, the journal is gone. Four random words you can remember beat clever symbols you cannot.

Why a journal needs a chain

A folder of screenshots proves almost nothing: files re-save, dates change, entries vanish. Magpie hashes every entry together with the one before it. Change anything, anywhere, and the final hash stops matching. Share that one hash early, with a friend, a lawyer, or your own email, and you can later prove the whole record existed exactly as it is now.

What it does

Everything encrypted

Entries and attachments are sealed with your passphrase on your device. Locked means unreadable, including to this app.

Exports that prove themselves

One zip: your files, the chain manifest, and a small python script anyone can run to verify the whole record without trusting Magpie.

Nothing leaves your device

No accounts, no cloud, no analytics. The Android app has no internet permission at all, which you can check in the manifest.

What it is not

  • Not a promise of court admissibility. The chain makes tampering evident; what a court accepts depends on much more. Talk to a lawyer for legal disputes.
  • Not proof of time by itself. Timestamps come from your device clock. Sharing the chain head early is what pins the record to a moment.
  • Not cloud backup. The journal lives only on this device; export regularly and keep copies somewhere safe. Uninstalling deletes everything.
  • Not for hostile-device situations. If someone controls your phone or knows your passphrase, no app protects the record.

The Android app cannot phone home

Magpie for Android requests no permissions. Photos come in through the system camera, and the operating system will not let the app open a network connection, ever.
